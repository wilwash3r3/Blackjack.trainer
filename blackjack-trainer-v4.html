The trade you're learning

Every dollar of edge you take costs you cover. The counter who bets perfectly gets barred; the counter who hides perfectly makes nothing. These are the working rules this drill grades against:

Heat ≥ 78% → leave, whatever the count
40+ rounds at one table → leave
Spread already ≥ 8× → hold, don't widen
TC ≥ 2, heat low → ramp one step (~2×)
TC ≥ 5, fresh table, min bet → a bigger step is fair
TC ≤ 1 → no edge, no raise
TC ≤ 1 and heat building → a cover bet buys room

Bet spread reference

Edge uses the standard 6-deck/S17 Hi-Lo rule of thumb: roughly −0.5% at true count 0, moving about 0.5% per true-count point (6:5 tables run about 1.4 points worse). SD/hand uses the widely-cited flat-bet benchmark of ≈1.14 (variance ≈1.3) per one-unit bet, held constant across counts as an approximation. App ramp is this trainer's built-in bet spread. Kelly units is the mathematically optimal bet at that count for your entered bankroll — full Kelly carries roughly a 13.5% risk of ruin on its own, so most professional counters bet half-Kelly or less for comfort.

Risk of Ruin uses the standard fixed-unit diffusion approximation RoR ≈ e^(−2·μ·B/σ²), where μ is expected profit per hand, B is your bankroll, and σ² is variance per hand — all in dollars. 🃏 How to Play

Place a bet with the −25 / +25 / +100 chips, then hit Deal. You'll get two cards face up; the dealer gets one up and one hidden. Hit, Stand, Double, or Split using the buttons (or keyboard shortcuts — see below). The dealer plays after you, standing on all 17s. Payouts follow whatever you've set in Options (3:2 or 6:5 blackjack).

Hitting into exactly 21 auto-stands for you — there's never a reason to hit again from there, so it skips the extra click.

🎯 Basic Strategy & Deviation Play

Basic Strategy always shows the mathematically correct play for your hand vs. the dealer's up card, assuming no count information. Toggle it off if you want to test yourself without a safety net.

Deviation Play shows when the true count is extreme enough that the correct play actually changes from basic strategy — these are called "index plays." For example, 16 vs. a dealer 10 is normally a Hit, but at a true count of 0 or higher, Stand is correct instead. This box tells you when a deviation applies, what the trigger threshold is, and what basic strategy alone would have said.

🔢 Card Counting (Hi-Lo)

This trainer uses the Hi-Lo counting system: cards 2–6 are worth +1, 7–9 are worth 0, and 10/face cards/Aces are worth −1. Add up every card you see to get the running count. Divide the running count by the number of decks remaining in the shoe to get the true count, which is what actually matters for betting and deviation decisions in a multi-deck game.

Turn on "Show card values" to see the +1/0/−1 value printed right on each card — useful while you're first learning the system. Turn on "Reveal running & true count" to check your mental math, or leave it off and use the Check boxes to quiz yourself blind.

The dealer's hidden hole card is never counted until it's actually flipped face up, matching what a real player at the table would know.

💰 Bet Spread & EV

Suggested bet uses a standard bet spread (1 unit at true count ≤1, up to 8 units at true count 5+, where 1 unit = $25) — this is the classic "bet more when the count is good" strategy. It has its own toggle so you can practice bet sizing without necessarily revealing the raw count number.

Current edge is a simplified estimate of your advantage or disadvantage right now, based on the true count and payout rules. Theoretical EV tracks what your accumulated edge times your bets predicted you'd win or lose across the session; Actual result is your real bankroll change. Comparing the two is a good way to see variance in action — you can play "perfectly" and still lose a session; that's normal.

🪑 AI Opponents & Seats

Turn order follows a real table: the seat on the dealer's left — the far right seat on screen — acts first, then play moves right to left around the arc, so the AI players to your right act before you and the ones to your left act after. The table has 6 seats total, including yours. Set how many AI opponents play (0–5) in Options — they bet, play basic strategy, and settle automatically each hand, either animated one at a time or instantly (also an Options toggle). Whoever's actually taking their turn — AI or you — gets shown bigger and highlighted; everyone else stays compact.

You can pick your own seat: any empty seat shows a "Sit here" button during betting. AI players occasionally cash out and leave, and new ones sit down at open seats between hands. Use 🚶 Move Tables during betting to leave for a fresh shoe and an entirely new set of opponents. Every so often (roughly every 20–35 hands) a dealer shift change happens on its own, bringing a fresh shoe with it — a nice forcing function to practice resetting your count.

🧪 Training Modes

Strategy Drills — random hard-total, soft-total, and pair scenarios (toggle categories on/off) with instant feedback and an explanation for every decision, plus an accuracy/streak tracker.

Count Practice — cards stream in one at a time with the count hidden. After a set number of cards you're quizzed on the running count (and optionally true count). Turn on hand-holding mode to watch the count update live instead of being quizzed.

Heat & Camo — bet-sizing scenarios instead of hands. You're given a true count, your last bet, the pit's attention, your spread so far and how long you've been sitting, and asked what to put out: hold flat, ramp a step, push hard, or leave. Every answer explains which factor decided it. This is the half of counting that math alone won't teach — the trade between taking edge and spending cover.

Deviations — the same idea as Strategy Drills, but specifically for count-based index plays: you're given a hand, a dealer up card, and a true count, and asked whether the deviation applies.

📊 Stats

Tracks hands played, your win/loss/push record, win rate, blackjacks, busts, total wagered, net result, and win/loss streaks — all session-only, resetting if you reload the page.

Basic Strategy Adherence compares every Hit/Stand/Double/Split you actually make against what basic strategy recommends at that exact moment — this is the number to watch if you want to know whether you're really learning the strategy chart or just winging it. Count-Check Accuracy does the same for your running/true count guesses.

🎴 The Shoe & Discard Tray

Both are live. The stack in the shoe on the right shrinks as cards come out; the discard tray on the left fills by the same amount. The red sliver in the shoe is the cut card — when the stack falls to it, the dealer shuffles. That's your penetration: the deeper the cut card sits, the more of the shoe you get to count, and the more your count is worth.

At a real table nobody tells you how many decks are left — you glance at the discard tray and estimate. Turn on Hide the shoe card count in Options and the readout up top disappears, replaced by a "Decks remaining?" check in the Card Count panel. Guess from the tray, then check yourself; within half a deck is good enough for an accurate true count, and Stats tracks your accuracy. This is the single most-skipped skill in learning to count, and it's the one that makes your true count real instead of theoretical.

⏱ Pace, the Clock & Distractions

Counting at your own speed is maybe 40% of the job. Three Options exist to take that comfort away:

These three settings apply to Play. Count Practice is a clean-room drill — it sets its own card speed with the Speed slider in that mode, and never interrupts you.

Table pace — Relaxed, Casino, or Crowded/fast. Casino is roughly a real six-deck table; Crowded is a full table with a fast dealer. Practice at Relaxed until you can hold a count, then never go back to it.

Decision clock — 12, 8, or 5 seconds of dealer patience. Run out and the hand auto-stands, logged in Stats as a timeout. Real dealers don't wait while you recompute a true count; if your timeouts are high, your count isn't automatic yet.

Table chatter — the dealer calling the game, players talking, a server offering drinks, popping up mid-hand. It is deliberately annoying. Holding a running count through noise while deciding a marginal 16 is what the casino floor actually feels like.

🎨 Deck Backs & Felt

💾 Saving & Devices

What gets saved: your skills — accuracy and streaks across all six drills — plus milestones earned and session stats. It saves automatically as you play; there is no save button to forget. Bankroll and the shoe are per-session and start fresh each time.

Where it lives: in this browser's own storage, on this device. That means it works offline, but it is not synced between your phone, tablet and computer, and it is not permanent.

On iPhone and iPad Safari deletes this kind of storage after roughly seven days without opening the page. Private tabs keep nothing at all, and "Clear History and Website Data" wipes it immediately. Android is more forgiving — Chrome has no seven-day timer — but clearing site data still erases it.

The only durable copy is the one you make: Export save file in the Stats tab downloads a small JSON file you can back up or carry to another machine, and Import save file brings it back. If you train on an iPad, export every few sessions.

Adding it to a home screen (iOS: Share → Add to Home Screen) makes it open like an app and exempts it from the seven-day timer — but the home-screen copy keeps its own separate progress. Export from Safari and import into it once, or pick one and stay there.

🛡 Insurance & Surrender

Insurance is offered whenever the dealer's up-card is an ace, before anyone plays. It costs half your wager and pays 2 to 1 if the dealer has blackjack. Played blind it is a losing bet — that is why the casino offers it. But it is also the single most valuable count-based decision in Hi-Lo: the bet wins when the hole card is a ten, so once the shoe is rich enough in tens the odds tip in your favour. The index is a true count of +3 — decline below it, take it at or above. Your calls are graded against that index and counted with your other deviations in Stats.

Surrender is off by default because plenty of tables don't offer it. Switch it on in Options and you may give up your first two cards for half your bet back — correct on 16 against a 9, 10 or ace, and 15 against a 10. It is not available after you hit, and not on a split hand.

🔥 Heat & Camouflage

Counting is legal; casinos are private property and can refuse your action. Almost nobody gets caught doing the arithmetic — they get caught by their bet spread. The Heat panel models the pit's attention on a 0–100% scale, from Cold through Noticed and Watched to Heat.

What raises it: jumping your bet 2× or 3× in one hand; a spread wider than about 8× at one table; putting a big bet out at exactly the moment the true count spikes; sitting at one table too long; being up a lot of money; leaving the table right when the count goes good (wonging out — real, effective, and very visible).

What lowers it: ramping in small steps; flat-betting through a good count; making a deliberate cover play — a big bet into a bad count. Cover costs real EV. That trade is the whole game at the professional level, and the panel names the ceiling you can bet each hand without spiking attention.

What happens if you ignore it: around 72% the dealer starts shuffling early, cutting your penetration and quietly killing your edge. Above 92% you are barred — your name goes in the book and the session ends. There is no sliding to the next table once your face is known. Peak heat and back-offs are tracked in Stats. Learning to move before that happens is the actual skill.

None of this makes the swings smaller. Even played perfectly, Hi-Lo on a six-deck shoe is roughly a 0.5–1% edge, and the variance dwarfs it over any short session.

⚙ Options

Decks per shoe (1–8), blackjack payout (3:2 standard or 6:5, a worse rule some casinos use), number of AI opponents (0–5), whether AI turns animate or resolve instantly, table pace, decision clock, table chatter, hiding the shoe readout, and pit heat. Applying options reshuffles the shoe; if a hand is mid-play, it's voided back to betting with no bankroll penalty.
